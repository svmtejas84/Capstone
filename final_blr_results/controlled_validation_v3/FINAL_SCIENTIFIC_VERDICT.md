# Controlled validation v3 — final scientific verdict

**Status:** The bounded Bengaluru campaign completed its timestamp-causal data repair, 415 valid validation-selected fits, one-time evaluation on the previously exposed test period, frozen paired analyses, and saved-prediction verification. All findings below are **exploratory**. They are not independent temporal, spatial, operational, or physical confirmation.

## Data eligibility and scope

The initially proposed saved array failed a full raw-data future-mutation test: a station reading after a forecast issue hour could enter that hour because raw timestamps were floored. Twenty selection and 54 final fits were stopped, quarantined, and never test-scored. `PROTOCOL_AMENDMENT_02.md` froze a repaired array that delays pollutant input rows and the residual anchor strictly before issue. The full mutation test passed on this array (SHA-256 `bd08d733f924387da82e758d1060703dfcdd121f360b7e8765189d8b7446e202`). Meteorological archive records are timestamped no later than each feature hour, but actual weather publication and station ingestion latencies are not available. Thus the protocol is **timestamp-causal retrospective historical-observation prediction**, not certified live forecasting. Source: `DATA_ELIGIBILITY.md`, `verification/full_future_mutation.json`, `data_manifests/source_checksums.csv`.

The saved array ends at 2026-03-28 23:00 UTC. The raw tail is too short for a new meaningful holdout. The September 2025–March 2026 test interval had been examined in earlier Bengaluru research. Its 14,583 observed station-hour target pairs were scored at each of 1, 3, 6, 12 and 24 hours. The training target coverage is 7.105% of 15-station hours; validation 24.106%; test 21.662%. For the six-hour scored input windows, 84.9% of pollutant input cells in training and 76.7% in test are imputed according to the repaired provenance mask. Mean observed PM2.5 is 24.970 µg/m³ in validation and 44.426 µg/m³ in test; changing station/time coverage also contributes to this aggregate shift. Source: `metrics/data_coverage_shift.csv`.

Eleven stations contributed test targets and four had none; this is 14,583 repeated station-hour measurements, not 14,583 independent spatial sites. Per-station split counts and exact scored eligibility are in `data_manifests/station_eligibility.csv`.

## RQ1 — forecasting architecture: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED descriptively

All newly fitted neural models received the same repaired 18-channel, 12-hour station tensor and were scored on the same 14,583 target pairs at each horizon. The corrected GINEConv–GRU with no regularizer reached ensemble MAE **10.896, 13.668, 15.494, 15.378, 14.304 µg/m³** at 1, 3, 6, 12 and 24 hours. The true-wind variant reached **11.161, 13.624, 15.366, 15.422, 14.597**. TCN reached **10.931, 13.649, 15.800, 15.642, 14.441**; MLP reached **10.997, 13.660, 15.792, 16.105, 14.375**. The same-hour-yesterday control reached **14.130** at 1–12 hours and **14.972** at 24 hours. Its 24-hour value uses a strictly prior causal anchor and is identical to the persistence implementation at that horizon.

This is evidence that the GINEConv–GRU can perform competitively under a matched **information set**. It does not show uniform superiority: yesterday's value outperforms all newly fitted neural models at 3, 6 and 12 hours, and the adaptive graph or unregularized backbone performs better at 24 hours than the true-wind model. Architectures have different capacities and validation-selected learning rates, so the comparison is not an isolated proof that graph message passing is necessary. All 16 model/control rows at each horizon, including weak baselines and negative R² values, are retained in `metrics/T2_MATCHED_FORECASTING.csv`; raw paired predictions are in `predictions/`.

## RQ2 — directional versus no regularization: NOT SUPPORTED as a general improvement

The five-seed mean-prediction ensembles used matched initialization, backbone, information, batch order and learning rate within each horizon. True-wind minus λ=0 paired MAE differences were **+0.265, −0.044, −0.129, +0.044, +0.292 µg/m³** at 1, 3, 6, 12, 24 hours; negative favors directional regularization. The six-hour exploratory seven-day interval is **[−0.404, +0.092]** and crosses zero. At one hour directional regularization is worse, interval **[+0.111, +0.405]**, exploratory Holm-adjusted block sign-flip p = **0.00784**. The 24-hour interval **[+0.008, +0.590]** also favors no regularization, though its Holm-adjusted p is 0.593. Three of five seeds favored true wind at six hours, exact two-sided sign p = 1.0. Source: `statistical_analysis/primary_contrasts.csv`, `statistical_analysis/seed_sign_tests.csv`.

## RQ3 — actual wind direction versus false wind and smoothing: NOT SUPPORTED as physical specificity

True wind did **not** consistently outperform the within-month shuffled-wind control: true-minus-shuffled ensemble differences were **−0.044, +0.037, −0.024, +0.043, +0.036 µg/m³** across the five horizons; none of these five contrasts had Holm-adjusted block sign-flip p below 0.05. True wind beat +90° rotated wind at 3, 6 and 12 hours in this exploratory analysis, with differences **−0.069, −0.137, −0.200** and adjusted p **0.00656, 0.00690, 0.00340**. It also beat the Laplacian comparator at 6, 12 and 24 hours, but Laplacian was worse than λ=0 at those three horizons. The Laplacian penalty had a higher mean weighted magnitude at selected epochs (~0.031) than true-wind directional (~0.018), and applies to more arcs, so beating it cannot establish a physically specific wind effect. Source: `statistical_analysis/primary_contrasts.csv`, `metrics/training_diagnostics.csv`.

Using the actual-wind cone with threshold 0.05 in normalized target units, the descriptive six-hour violation rates on arcs with **both endpoint targets observed** were 24.98% true wind and 24.60% λ=0, among 5,817 eligible arcs. Rates for every regularizer and horizon are in `metrics/wind_cone_diagnostics.csv`. This diagnostic population is narrower than the all-node training penalty and cannot establish atmospheric correctness. No conservation equation, emissions balance, or validated roadside concentration follows from the hinge.

## RQ4 — optimization and effective regularization: PARTIALLY RESOLVED, no robust benefit

The main regularizer comparison used one seed-11 validation-selected learning rate per horizon, fixed across all variants and seeds: **0.001** at one hour and **0.0003** at 3, 6, 12 and 24 hours. All 100 regularized final checkpoints were selected at or after epoch 10, after the frozen five-epoch warmup had reached the full nominal λ = **0.06**. The corresponding 25 unregularized checkpoints have λ = 0. This removes the historical mismatch in learning-rate policy and early checkpoint exposure to a still-warming penalty. It does not force equal penalty gradients or equal stopping epochs. At selected epochs the mean unweighted penalty was ~0.306 for true wind versus ~0.517 for Laplacian; mean weighted values were ~0.018 and ~0.031. All four nonzero-regularization conditions had zero batches with no effective arcs across their saved training histories, but their active-arc totals differ. The mixed and unfavorable predictive effects remain under this matched policy. Source: `metrics/training_diagnostics.csv`, `metrics/resource_usage.csv`, `figures/F4_effective_lambda.pdf`.

## RQ5 — independent generalization: NOT EVALUATED

No untouched sufficiently long later Bengaluru test interval was available. No new fold-isolated leave-one-station-out preprocessing and training was run; the saved full-station array cannot certify held-out-site isolation. The prior negative spatial-transfer evidence remains historical context, not a new v3 result. No second-city, external, roadside or operational validation was performed. A timestamp-causal array and a successful V100 run do not make these results prospective.

## Statistical and resource integrity

The 20 frozen ensemble contrasts use exact station/time pairing, 2,000 seven-observed-day moving-block bootstrap draws for **unadjusted exploratory intervals**, and a separate 100,000-draw nonoverlapping seven-day block sign-flip test with Holm adjustment over the full family. Seven of 20 exploratory adjusted p-values are below 0.05; this count includes unfavorable evidence (the one-hour true-wind degradation) and does not override repeated test exposure or exchangeability assumptions. The five-seed sign tests are separate and low-powered. No outcome was used to drop a horizon or seed. `scripts/verify_results.py` independently recomputed all **355** saved metric rows and all **20** paired contrast rows, confirmed all seven frozen source hashes, and checked 14,583 matched pairs per horizon (`verification/results_verification.json`).

The V100 completed 415 valid fit cells in **1.405 recorded fit hours**, plus **0.541 hours** for the 74 quarantined fits; setup and orchestration time are additional. One-batch allocated GPU peak was **1.21 GB**; a live training measurement showed **1512 MiB** GPU memory in use. Training used one supervised worker at a time. No resource ceiling was exceeded. Checkpoint hashes, epochs, validation selection and repaired-array identity passed the saved-cell verifiers. Full compute accounting is in `metrics/resource_usage.csv`, `verification/smoke_gpu.json`, supervisor logs and cell records.

## Claims and next evidence

Supported within this bounded exploratory setting: timestamp-causal historical-observation input construction after the explicit pollutant-hour repair; complete matched regularizer training and scoring; a competitive unregularized GINEConv–GRU at some horizons; deterministic statistical and artifact regeneration. Mixed: small horizon-dependent wind-regularization differences. Unsupported: a consistent directional benefit, wind-specific predictive advantage across shuffled controls, mass conservation, operational availability, spatial transfer or independently validated road-level accuracy. Independent prospective temporal data with issue-time weather provenance, a genuinely isolated station evaluation, and physically measured roadside data are needed for broader claims. The v3 results must not be presented as independent confirmation in a manuscript.

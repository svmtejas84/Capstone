# Capacity and allocation validation

Two different algorithms are present and must not be merged conceptually.

1. `scripts/evaluate_routing_gs.py::deferred_acceptance` reserves every directed edge in a commuter's selected route, checks per-edge quotas, releases an entire bundle on rejection, and asserts at termination that all retained edge occupancies are within the supplied quota. The historical simulation completed successfully, so the assertion passed for that run. However, the archived summary does not contain a separate edge occupancy/capacity audit, exact edge quotas, route paths, or an independently recomputable violation count. Report “zero violations according to the allocator's terminal assertion,” not a separately audited empirical capacity guarantee. Its quotas are pollution-inverse heuristic values, not measured traffic limits.
2. `matcher/gale_shapley.py::batch_match`, called by the serving API, sees a capacity per candidate route only. It has no edge/path membership input. Two candidate routes can share the same road segment and both admit a commuter even if that segment has capacity one. Its fallback also appends unmatched commuters without verifying route quota. It therefore does not guarantee shared-edge or even route-capacity feasibility in all fallback cases.

The deterministic toy graph diagnostic `ROUTING_TOY_DIAGNOSTICS.json` passes the offline bundle quota (1 user on a shared edge with quota 1) and constructs a serving-API counterexample assigning two commuters to two routes that share one unrepresented edge. That is the minimum correction evidence: capacity-aware service must use edge-resource bundles and must never use a fallback that violates quotas; or solve a separate constrained assignment/flow problem. The offline bundle allocator is reusable as a bounded heuristic, but it is not a classical stable matching for complementarities and gives no welfare-optimality guarantee.

`matcher.road_capacity.route_capacity` uses nominal road-class VPH per lane, parsed/default lanes or width, optional congestion, 900-second window and route-duration adjustment, with a minimum capacity of one. These values are assumptions and do not encode observed Bengaluru flow. A genuine route-capacity claim needs validated traffic flow/time windows, shared-edge conflict definitions, and explicit capacity audits. No new allocator was added to production.

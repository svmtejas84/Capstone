## 1. Research object and boundaries

ST-PIGNN forecasts monitored Bengaluru PM2.5 with a spatial-temporal neural backbone and a weak upwind-inspired loss. The corrected station model uses a **15-station directed four-nearest-neighbor distance graph**, 12 hourly inputs with 18 channels, two GINE spatial layers, a GRU temporal encoder, and a residual forecast over a persistence anchor; the original corrected model has 43,393 parameters. The physics loss is a wind-cone hinge on selected directed station edges. It does **not** implement full advection–diffusion, measured emissions Q, or mass conservation. Sources: `gnn/model.py`, `src/stpignn/models_v2.py`, `src/stpignn/physics_v2.py`, `scripts/benchmark_v2j.py`, `final_blr_results/physics_audit/methodology/PHYSICS_ATTRIBUTION_METHODOLOGY.md`.

The separate **154,902-node OSM-derived road graph** is an engineering/navigation asset, not the model's 15-station training graph. Raw `data/graphs/bangalore_utm_{nodes,edges}.parquet` has 393,089 directed edge rows; `data/processed/graph/topology_graph.pt` has 424,550 symmetrized/deduplicated message-passing arcs. The graph is structurally connected, but its static 17-channel node tensor and 16-channel road edge vectors do not match the station model's 18 dynamic channels and distance edge features. Sixty-four partitions omit 7,134 cross-boundary arcs. Twenty-three station-to-road mappings exist, but only 22 nodes have stored node-hour rows; 154,880 nodes lack them. See `final_blr_results/road_graph/`.

The original notebook/navigation path included a citywide ST-PIGNN checkpoint, a road-graph feature builder, exposure-aware routing and A* related claims. Canonical audits found an untrained historical baseline, mismatched routing feature scaling, missing paired legacy baseline predictions and failed routing validation. The old saved routing field ranged roughly 1.28–1.63 µg/m³ and is not validated citywide concentration; reported dose/time route gains are unverified. `citywide_stpignn_best.pt` exists at repository root but is a legacy pathway checkpoint, not a corrected v2j checkpoint. See `results/research_audit_r1/bengaluru_verification.md`, `final_blr_results/archive/historical_results_register.md`, `scripts/prepare_routing_snapshot.py`, and `scripts/evaluate_routing_gs.py`. Structural graph validity does not imply road-node forecast accuracy or routing effectiveness.

## 3. Current scientific conclusions

- The 15-station ST-PIGNN has **exploratory intermediate-horizon** advantages over some historical neural baselines under matched v2j evaluation. There is no statistically established advantage over the new TCN; the new models have a different weather information set. The h6/h12 ST-PIGNN-versus-MLP contrasts are exploratory cross-protocol associations, not isolated architectural effects.
- Weak physics regularization has mixed, horizon-dependent predictive benefit. Ensemble averaging drives h1 and much of h3; h6 is more seed-consistent. Earlier W7 larger-λ trends were negative and are preserved. The hinge neither conserves mass nor implements full advection–diffusion; no measured Q is available. Physics specificity beyond generic Laplacian smoothing is unproven.
- Corrected W6 LOSO has no neural WIN. Station-graph LOSO evidence does not establish road-node spatial transfer. Road-node accuracy, citywide pollution maps and exposure-aware routing effectiveness remain unvalidated. No fabricated labels or imputed road-node targets may close those gaps.
- v2j local-time timestamp correction was necessary but its centered hourly feature included a post-cutoff observation. v2m uses cutoff-safe c−30 weather, so cross-round model rankings do not measure only architecture.
